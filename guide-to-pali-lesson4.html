Grammar

The Ablative Case
Separation & Origin

The Ablative case indicates separation or origin — it translates as "from" or "away from." It shows where someone or something comes from, falls from, or departs from.

The Ablative has multiple singular endings — all equally correct: -ā, -smā, -mhā. The plural shares the form -ehi with the Instrumental.

For masculine nouns ending in -a:

Singular: base + -ā / -smā / -mhā

Plural:   base + -ehi

Examples

Sentences with four cases

Dārako pāsādā patati.
The child falls from the palace.
Naro gāmasmā nikkhamati.
The man leaves from the village.
Amacco pāsādamhā otarati.
The minister descends from the palace.
Kumārā rukkhehi patanti.
Boys fall from the trees.
Suko rukkhasmā patati.
The parrot falls from the tree.
