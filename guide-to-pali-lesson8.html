Quick Revision — All Seven Cases

You've mastered seven cases for masculine -a nouns! This lesson completes the paradigm with the Vocative and introduces Neuter nouns.

Connection: The Vocative is the simplest case — the base form itself for singular. Combined with neuter nouns, you'll complete all the foundational patterns of Pāli nouns.
1Name all seven cases you've learned.→ Nom, Acc, Ins, Abl, Dat, Gen, Loc
2What is the Locative plural ending?→ -esu
3Which cases share the plural ending -ehi?→ Instrumental and Ablative

Grammar — Part A

The Vocative Case
Direct Address

The Vocative is used for direct address — calling to someone. In English: "O man!" or just "Man!"

For masculine nouns ending in -a:

Singular: bare base | nara! (O man!)

Plural:   base + -ā | narā! (O men!)

The Vocative singular is just the uninflected base — the simplest case! The plural is -ā (same as Nominative plural).

Grammar — Part B

Neuter Nouns in -a

Neuter nouns follow the same pattern as masculine nouns for most cases. They differ only in the Nominative, Vocative, and Accusative:

Neuter -a nouns differ in these three cases:

Nom/Voc/Acc Singular: base + -aṃ | phalaṃ (fruit)

Nom/Voc/Acc Plural:   base + -āni | phalāni (fruits)

Common neuter nouns: phala (fruit), nayana (eye), nagara (city), udaka (water), citta (mind).

Examples

Vocative & Neuter in sentences

Nara, dhammaṃ rakkhāhi!
O man, protect the doctrine!
Kumāra, rukkhaṃ āruhāhi!
O boy, climb the tree!
Kumārā, gāmaṃ gacchatha!
O boys, go to the village!
Naro phalaṃ passati.
The man sees a fruit.
Kumāro phalāni āharati.
The boy brings fruits.
