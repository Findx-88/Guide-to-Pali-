Grammar

The Dative Case
Purpose & Recipient

The Dative case indicates the indirect object — the person for whom or to whom something is done. It also marks purpose.

Dative answers: For whom? / To whom? / For what purpose?

For masculine nouns ending in -a:

Singular: base + -āya or -ssa

Plural:   base + -ānaṃ

Examples

Expressing purpose & recipient

Kassako tāpasāya bhattaṃ āharati.
The farmer brings rice for the hermit.
Ācariyassa kumāro mañcaṃ āharati.
The boy brings a bed for the teacher.
Luddako sīhānaṃ aje rakkhati.
The hunter protects goats from lions.
Samaṇānaṃ kumārā odanaṃ pacanti.
Boys cook rice for the monks.
Vejjassa naro lābhaṃ āharati.
The man brings profit for the doctor.
