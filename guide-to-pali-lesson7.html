Quick Revision — Lesson VI (Genitive)

The Genitive (-ssa sg., -ānaṃ pl.) shows possession: "of the man," "the farmer's son."

Connection: The Genitive tells us who owns something. The Locative tells us where something is. "The man's son sits in the monastery."
1What is the Genitive singular ending?→ -ssa
2Which two cases share -ssa and -ānaṃ?→ Dative and Genitive

Grammar

The Locative Case
Location

The Locative case indicates where something happens. It translates as "in," "on," "at," or "upon."

For masculine nouns ending in -a:

Singular: base + -smiṃ or -e | narasmiṃ / nare

Plural:   base + -esu | naresu

Seven cases — almost complete!

Examples

Location in sentences

Samaṇo vihārasmiṃ nisīdati.
The monk sits in the monastery.
Migā pabbatesu vasanti.
Deer live in the mountains.
Kumāro rukkhe nisīdati.
The boy sits on/in the tree.
Macchā dīpesu vasanti.
Fish live on the islands.
Ācariyo pāsādasmiṃ vasati.
The teacher lives in the palace.
