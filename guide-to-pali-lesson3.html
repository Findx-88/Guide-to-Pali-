Quick Revision — Lessons I & II

You know two cases: Nominative (-o/-ā) marks the subject and Accusative (-ṃ/-e) marks the object. Together they form: Subject + Object + Verb.

Connection: The Instrumental adds a third dimension to your sentences. Now you can say by what means an action is done: "The man goes by chariot."
1What are the Accusative endings for masc. -a nouns?→ -ṃ (sg.) / -e (pl.)
2"The king goes to the village" — translate to Pāli.→ Bhūpālo gāmaṃ gacchati
3What does the Accusative case mark?→ The direct object or goal of motion

Grammar

The Instrumental Case
Agent, Means & Accompaniment

The Instrumental case expresses the means or agent by which an action is done. It translates as "by," "with," or "by means of."

Think of it as answering: By what? / With what? / Together with whom?

For masculine nouns ending in -a:

Singular: base + -ena | nara + ena = narena

Plural:   base + -ehi | nara + ehi = narehi

Examples

Three-case sentences

Naro rathena gāmaṃ gacchati.
The man goes to the village by chariot.
Kassako hatthena rukkhaṃ paharati.
The farmer hits the tree with his hand.
Kumārā assehi gāmaṃ gacchanti.
Boys go to the village by horses.
Sāvako pādena maggaṃ gacchati.
The disciple goes on the path on foot.
Samaṇā sāvakehi saggaṃ gacchanti.
Monks go to heaven with disciples.
