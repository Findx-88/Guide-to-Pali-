Grammar — Part A

Nouns in -a

The Nominative Case is used to indicate the subject of a sentence. In Pāli, words change their endings to show their grammatical role. For masculine nouns ending in -a, the base drops the -a and adds an ending.

For masculine nouns ending in -a:

Singular: base + -o | The man = naro

Plural:   base + -ā | Men / The men = narā

Base: nara (man)

Nominativenaronarā

Grammar — Part B

Verbs — Present Tense

Like nouns, verbs change ending depending on who is performing the action (I, you, he/they). For now we will focus on the third person (he/she/it/they).

For third person present tense verbs:

Singular: root + -ti | He goes = gacchati

Plural:   root + -nti | They go = gacchanti

Base: gaccha (go, from √gam)
